Where we agree

Penalized logistic regression is the right prospect model. All four of us ended on a regularized logistic regression or a simple ranking. Flexible trees failed or overfit without heavy regularization: Erin's untuned histogram boosting reached 33.6%, Samantha's shallow tree 1.7%, and in Benjamin's screen LightGBM, histogram boosting, and the single tree fell below the bar, with XGBoost catching up only once tuning forced shallow, heavily regularized trees. Kate's tuned LightGBM only tied her logistic model.
Giving history carries the signal. Solicitation and Passport viewing added nothing measurable in any notebook, whether tested in the same year (Benjamin, Kate) or strictly before the snapshot (Samantha: +0.7 points, −3.2 to 5.0).
The model helps overall, mostly by ranking former major donors. Erin's list composition shows it directly: her logistic list captured 74 of 75 former-major converters against 67 for prior-year giving, and 34 of 59 never-major converters against 34. Kate found the extra converters were $100 to $250 donors with a past major year, a soft credit, or a long record. Benjamin's drivers point the same way (former major status, largest gift).
Never-major donors are not reliably improved at 12 months. Benjamin +4.8, Erin −0.3, and Samantha +3.2 all have intervals that include zero. Kate's +9 is the exception, but it uses a different population and test years.
Probabilities rank well but their level drifts. Every notebook found the top of the list roughly calibrated and the year totals off: Erin's FY2025 check predicted 0.108% against 0.246% observed, Benjamin's predicted 48% of FY2025 conversions, and Kate's yearly totals ran 0.67 to 1.57 times expected.
The tier cannot be predicted beyond "most land at $1,200". Kate (53% for always guessing the lowest tier, nothing better) and Benjamin (59%, multinomial measurably worse) agree.
Retention risk can be ranked. The two notebooks that tried it, Samantha's and Benjamin's, both beat their simple benchmarks on the same donors.

What each of us concluded

Benjamin

The tuned L1 logistic regression beats the prior-year ranking and the former major or $500 rule, though the cleaner pre-program test suggests a gain of a few points rather than seven. Never-major donors are not reliably helped at 12 months; the 5-year boosting machine is the only model that clears that bar (+2.2). Major donors who drop give up about as much a year as new major donors give, so retention is the first test recommended to PBS, followed by a backtest on its own records, contact logging with a holdout, and yearly capacity snapshots.

Erin

Untuned logistic regression is selected on its overall recall. Its gain comes from former-major capture, not never-major outreach, and the probabilities are uncalibrated and should be used for ranking only. The final model is saved, reload-tested, and has scored FY2026; household de-duplication and staff review are needed before any use.

Kate

Logistic regression beats both simple rules modestly and helps most for never-major donors by lifting lower-giving donors with strong history above the $600 tier. Complexity does not pay, engagement adds nothing, a separate 3-year model cannot yet be tested, and the tier should be a two-level weak indicator. Next steps are uplift using the holdout and a look at why Unknown-gender records are over-represented on the list.

Samantha

PBS should run three workflows. For never-major prospects, prior-year giving remains the operational choice because ridge's gain was not reliable. For current majors, the giving-history ridge reliably ranks retention risk. For former majors, prior-year giving is enough. Every list is decision support to be validated against real outreach results.

What to synthesize, and why

Structure: Samantha's three workflows

Organize the group notebook around prospects, retention risk, and former-major reactivation. It matches what PBS's major gift officers asked for (retention), and Erin's composition result explains why it works: the overall gain is mostly former-major capture, so separating that group makes each list's value visible.

Data and rules: the shared build and pre-set rules

Use the group's shared build script for every model; Erin's rebuild already reproduces its numbers exactly, which confirms it. Kate's and Samantha's models should be rerun on it. Keep the practice of setting decision rules before each comparison, which Benjamin, Kate, and Erin all used in some form.

Validation: rolling test plus two checks

Rolling FY2022 to FY2024 (Benjamin, Erin, Samantha), the FY2025 check run once (Benjamin, Erin), and the FY2018 to FY2021 pre-program test that Jeff's notes call for (Benjamin). Report paired couple-clustered intervals (Benjamin, Erin) and add Kate's count of converters found by only one list, which is the easiest version for a non-technical audience.

Prospect model: one penalized logistic regression

All four agree on the family. Benjamin's tuned L1 and Erin's untuned L2 are within about two points, so pick one under a pre-set rule and show the other as a check. Drop the contact flags, since they change nothing. Add Kate's log-dollar and giving-band terms as the tested way to handle the $600 cluster.

Explaining the gain: Erin's and Kate's breakdowns

Erin's list composition (former versus never major) and Kate's band table (who the model finds that the baseline misses) together tell staff why the list differs from a dollar sort. Add Kate's 3-year total as a second simple baseline.

Retention: Samantha's capacity framing with Benjamin's risk list

Use Samantha's top-25 and top-50 evaluation, which matches how officers would work a short list, and report dollars exposed as she does. Add Benjamin's FY2026 risk list of current major donors as the deliverable.

Longer windows and tier

Use Benjamin's 3-year and 5-year models as projections, with Kate's finding that late converters look like everyone else as the caveat. Report the tier as Kate's two-level indicator or Benjamin's expected mix, since nobody could predict it.

Deliverables: Erin's artifact discipline

Ship the final model the way Erin did: a saved, hash-checked pipeline whose scoring always switches training-only inputs off. Pair it with Benjamin's working queue and Kate's per-donor drivers, expected-converter range, and gender coverage audit.

Proposed group notebook outline

  1. Introduction and data framing. Business problem, the three workflows, and Jeff's synthetic-data notes up front.
  2. Shared data and features. The group build script; giving-history features with Kate's bands; leakage checks.
  3. Benchmarks. Prior-year giving, former major or $500, 3-year total, and decline and recency for retention.
  4. Prospect model. Model families compared under one rule; never-major results reported separately; the gain explained through list composition and the band table.
  5. Validation checks. FY2025 once, pre-program FY2018 to FY2021, donors not selected for cultivation.
  6. Retention and reactivation. Current-major risk at officer capacity, with dollars at risk; former-major reactivation against prior-year giving.
  7. Longer windows and tier. As projections and an expected mix.
  8. FY2026 lists and model artifact. Working queue, risk list, saved model, coverage audit.
  9. Results and tests for PBS. Framed as methods PBS can run on its own records.
