Group Modeling Decisions

The choices the team has to make before building the group modeling notebook, why each one needs a decision, the options with their pros and cons, and a recommendation. Every option is checked against the group EDA's 15 agreed decisions and the revised Business Problem Statement (BPS).

The companion page, Team Modeling Synthesis, shows what each of us did. Three things have happened since the EDA and BPS were agreed, and several decisions turn on them: Jeff's October 30 data notes (the cultivation tables are invented and simulated contact shapes the FY2022 to FY2024 outcomes; age has no planted effect; second gifts are twice the real rate; results validate methods, not findings), PBS Utah's confirmation of the household rule, and PBS's request for a ranking of current major donors at risk of dropping.

Status, November 6: all 22 recommendations accepted. The Business Problem Statement may be revised to reflect the EDA and modeling, including the two amendments in D14 and D19.

Status, November 11: six amendments (D23 to D28) were added to the group modeling notebook after the instructor's review of the first complete draft. Each is dated, reported under the same paired test as every other comparison, and cannot reselect a model: a stronger unaided rule as the bar for the prospect list (D23) and for retention (D24), ranking by expected giving (D25), the list size read off a curve of return against size for prospects (D26) and for retention (D27), and the two curves side by side in dollars per name worked (D28). On this release the rules did as well as the models at every job, so the recommended lists are rule-ordered and the models supply probabilities and expected amounts alongside. The amendments are explained in the notebook's Introduction, under "Amendments after the results".

D1. Data build

Consistent

EDA and BPS: EDA decision 12 (one shared Python build), decisions 1 and 4 (content-based copy rules, legacy gifts to the first listed person). BPS Scope delivers "a cleaned donor-level dataset".

Shared group build script for everyone Recommended

Pros
  • One set of counts, bars, and definitions
  • Already reproduces the published EDA numbers
Cons
  • Kate and Samantha must rerun their models
  • Samantha's R work needs the panel exported to a file

Keep each person's pipeline

Pros
  • No rework
Cons
  • Results cannot be compared or combined
  • Breaks EDA decision 12

EDA and BPS: EDA decision 15; BPS Analytics Approach ("a couple whose combined giving reaches $1,200 counts as one major donor"). PBS has confirmed the rule.

Group rule Recommended

Cons
  • Affects only one couple-year, so it adds little

Reciprocal links only

Pros

D3. Who is eligible in the headline model

Consistent

Why: we used three populations. Erin and Benjamin used all active non-major donors with former majors flagged. Samantha modeled never-major donors only, and Kate included lapsed donors.

EDA and BPS: EDA decisions 2 and 3 (gave in FY t and not major; former majors kept and flagged; lapsed reported separately). BPS: "each current non-major donor", results "overall and for never-major donors".

All active non-major, former majors flagged Recommended

Pros
  • Matches the EDA and BPS and the 75.4% bar
  • Never-major results still reported separately
Cons
  • Former majors dominate the overall gain, which can hide the never-major result unless it is shown next to it

Never-major only (Samantha)

Pros

Cons
  • Drops about half the converters and the agreed bar
  • Former majors would need their own list

Include lapsed donors (Kate)

Pros
  • Can surface lapsed donors who come back
Cons
  • Lapsed donors rarely reach the top 1,000; breaks decision 2

Recommendation: keep decisions 2 and 3. Report lapsed donors with their base rate, and include Kate's lapsed-only list as an optional view.

D4. Ties at the list cutoff

Consistent

Why: many donors give exactly $600, so how ties are broken moves recall by a couple of points. Samantha's never-major baseline is 57.6% against the group's 59.6% on the same 59 converters, most likely for this reason.

EDA and BPS: EDA decision 10 ("ties at the cutoff are split evenly").

Split ties evenly Recommended

Pros
  • Agreed; independent of row order or a seed
Cons
  • Fractional counts are harder to explain than a list

Fixed random order (Kate)

Pros
  • Produces an actual list of names
Cons
  • Results depend on the seed; bars will not match

Recommendation: split evenly for evaluation, and use a fixed random order only to print the FY2026 list.

D8. How a model "wins"

Consistent

Why: Erin chose on the best point estimate, Kate on development PR-AUC with a sign test, and Benjamin and Samantha on paired bootstrap intervals. With 134 converters, a point or two is noise.

EDA and BPS: EDA decision 11 and the success section ("compare on the same bootstrap draws, not by checking whether two intervals overlap"). BPS: "several models will be compared".

Paired couple-clustered bootstrap; simplest model unless a gain's interval is above zero Recommended

Pros
  • Matches the EDA; guards against picking a lucky model
Cons
  • Will usually pick the simplest model, since most gains are within noise

Best point estimate

Pros
  • Simple to explain
Cons
  • Rewards noise with this few converters

Recommendation: the bootstrap rule, set in writing before results, with Kate's count of converters found by only one list as the plain-language version.
